Pork Adobo

Adobong Baboy

⏱ 45 min active 🕐 + marinate 30 min–overnight 🍚 Serves 4–6

Pork Adobo is the quintessential Filipino comfort food, celebrated as the country's unofficial national dish for its deeply savory, tangy, and subtly sweet flavor profile. The magic of this dish lies in its simple yet powerful braising liquid, made from a balanced blend of soy sauce, vinegar, plenty of crushed garlic, whole black peppercorns, and dried bay leaves. Marbled cuts of pork — most traditionally pork belly (liempo) or shoulder (kasim) — are simmered slowly in this marinade until the meat becomes incredibly tender, letting the savory-sour notes reach all the way to the bone while the fat renders into the sauce.

What truly elevates this dish is the technique: searing the marinated meat in hot oil before or after simmering creates caramelized edges. As the liquid reduces, the rendered fat merges with the soy-vinegar mixture into a rich, glossy sauce that's unmatched spooned over warm rice. Because vinegar is a natural preservative, Adobo famously tastes even better the next day, once the flavors have had time to meld.

Ingredients

The Meat & Marinade

  • 1 kg (2.2 lbs) pork — marbled cuts like belly (liempo), shoulder (kasim), or a mix of belly and ribs, cut into 2-inch chunks
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce — Filipino brands like Silver Swan or Datu Puti for the most authentic flavor
  • 1/3 cup cane or coconut vinegar — gives the dish its signature tang
  • 1 head garlic — cloves separated, peeled, and crushed
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns — slightly crushed to release their oils
  • 3–4 dried bay leaves (laurel) — adds a subtle earthy depth

For Searing & Simmering

  • 1–2 tbsp cooking oil — for searing the marinated pork
  • 1 cup water — to help simmer and tenderize without burning the sauce
  • 1–2 tsp brown sugar (optional) — balances the salt and vinegar

Optional Add-ins

  • 3–4 hard-boiled eggs — shelled, added late to soak up the sauce
  • 2 medium potatoes — peeled, quartered, and fried before adding

Method

Marinating

30 min – overnight
  1. Combine the pork chunks, soy sauce, crushed garlic, crushed peppercorns, and bay leaves in a large bowl or zip-top bag.
  2. Mix well to coat the meat evenly. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, or overnight for the deepest flavor. Do not add the vinegar yet.

Searing the Pork

10 min
  1. Remove the pork from the marinade, shaking off excess liquid. Save the marinade liquid and aromatics — don't throw them away.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Sear the pork in a single layer for 3–5 minutes until the edges are browned and caramelized.

Braising & Simmering

40 min
  1. Pour the reserved marinade, garlic, peppercorns, and bay leaves into the pot with the seared pork. Add 1 cup of water.
  2. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer for 30 minutes, or until the pork is tender.
  3. Add the vinegar (critical step): pour it in and do not stir. Let it boil uncovered for 3–5 minutes so the raw alcohol taste evaporates, leaving a smooth tang.
  4. Stir the pot gently. If using brown sugar, mix it in now to round out the salt and sour notes.

Reducing the Sauce

5 min
  1. For a thick, glossy sauce, remove the lid and raise the heat to medium. Let it reduce for about 5 minutes until the fat separates from the dark sauce. If using hard-boiled eggs, drop them in now to coat.

To serve: spoon hot over a generous bed of steamed white rice.
