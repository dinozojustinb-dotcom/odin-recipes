Pork Nilaga

Food Recipes

Description

Pork Nilaga (which translates literally to "Boiled Pork") is a traditional Filipino soup made by slow-cooking pork chunks until they are melt-in-your-mouth tender.

The clear, comforting broth is seasoned simply with fish sauce (patis), whole black peppercorns, and onions, allowing the natural savory flavor of the meat to take center stage. It is packed with a hearty variety of vegetables, typically including potatoes, saba bananas (which add a subtle sweetness), cabbage, and bok choy (pechay). Served piping hot alongside a bowl of steamed white rice and a side of fish sauce with a squeeze of calamansi, it is the ultimate Filipino comfort food, especially popular on rainy days or during cold weather.

    Ingredients

    The Meat and Broth Base

  • 1 kg (2.2 lbs) Pork – Use bone-in, collagen-rich cuts like pork hocks (pata), spare ribs, neck bones, or marbled belly (liempo) cut into 2-inch chunks.
  • 1.5 to 2 liters Water – Enough to completely submerge the meat and produce plenty of broth.
  • 1 medium Red Onion – Peeled and quartered.
  • 1 tbsp Whole Black Peppercorns (pamintang buo) – Essential for that distinct, peppery Nilaga warmth.
  • 2 to 3 tbsp Fish Sauce (patis) – To taste, for a savory umami base.

    • The Hearty Starches (Pick one or both)

  • 2 to 3 medium Potatoes – Peeled and quartered.
  • 2 to 3 pieces Saging na Saba (Cardaba Bananas) – Ripe but firm, cut in half. This adds a subtle, authentic sweetness to the broth.

    • The Fresh Vegetables

  • 1 small head Cabbage (repolyo) – Cut into quarters or wedges, with the core intact to keep leaves together.
  • 1 bunch Pechay (Bok Choy) – Leaves separated, washed, and bottom stalks trimmed.
  • 1 bunch Green Beans or Long Beans (baguio beans or sitaw) – Ends trimmed, snapped in half.

    • Seasonings

  • Salt – To taste, for final adjustments.
  • Fish Sauce (patis) and Calamansi – Placed on the side as a dipping sauce when serving.

    Step-by-Step

    Phase 1: Cleaning and Parboiling (10 minutes)

  1. Boil and drain: Place your pork chunks in a large pot and cover with water. Bring to a boil for 5 minutes.
  2. Wash the meat: Drain the cloudy water completely and rinse the pork chunks under cold tap water to wash away any clotted blood or scum. Clean the pot.

    3. Phase 2: Simmering the Broth (45 to 60 minutes)

  3. Build the base: Put the cleaned pork back into the pot. Add 1.5 to 2 liters of fresh water, the quartered onion, 1 tablespoon of whole black peppercorns, and 2 tablespoons of fish sauce (patis).
  4. Simmer until tender: Bring the liquid to a boil over high heat, then immediately lower the heat to a gentle simmer. Cover the pot and cook for 45 to 60 minutes (or longer if using pata) until the pork is fork-tender.
  5. Skim the top: Periodically scoop out any residual foam or excess oil floating on the surface to keep the broth clean.

    6. Phase 3: Cooking the Starches (10 to 12 minutes)

  6. Add potatoes and bananas: Toss in your quartered potatoes and the halved saging na saba (if using). Cover and simmer for about 10 minutes, or until a fork can easily pierce through the center of the potatoes.
  7. Taste check: Sip the broth. Adjust the seasoning by adding a pinch of salt or another splash of fish sauce according to your taste preference.

    8. Phase 4: Steaming the Leafy Greens (3 minutes)

  8. Layer the vegetables: Add the green beans, cabbage wedges, and pechay (bok choy) stalks into the pot, placing the delicate pechay leaves right on top.
  9. Final wilt: Do not stir. Cover the pot tightly and let it steam for 2 to 3 minutes until the vegetables turn a vibrant green but remain crisp. Turn off the heat.

    10. Ladle the hot soup, meat, and vegetables into large bowls. Serve immediately alongside a dish of warm white rice. Always provide a small saucer of fish sauce (patis) with a squeeze of calamansi or crushed bird's eye chili (siling labuyo) on the side for dipping.
