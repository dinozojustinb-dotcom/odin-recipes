Pork Nilaga

Nilagang Baboy

⏱ 70 min 🍚 Serves 4–6

Pork Nilaga (literally, "boiled pork") is a traditional Filipino soup made by slow-cooking pork chunks until they're melt-in-your-mouth tender. The clear, comforting broth is seasoned simply with fish sauce (patis), whole black peppercorns, and onions, letting the natural savoriness of the meat take center stage.

It's packed with a hearty mix of vegetables — typically potatoes, saba bananas for a subtle sweetness, cabbage, and bok choy (pechay). Served piping hot alongside steamed white rice and a side of fish sauce with calamansi, it's the ultimate comfort food, especially on rainy or cold days.

Ingredients

The Meat & Broth Base

  • 1 kg (2.2 lbs) pork — bone-in, collagen-rich cuts like hocks (pata), spare ribs, neck bones, or marbled belly (liempo), cut into 2-inch chunks
  • 1.5–2 liters water — enough to fully submerge the meat and give plenty of broth
  • 1 medium red onion — peeled and quartered
  • 1 tbsp whole black peppercorns — essential for that peppery warmth
  • 2–3 tbsp fish sauce (patis) — to taste, for a savory umami base

The Hearty Starches

  • 2–3 medium potatoes — peeled and quartered
  • 2–3 saba bananas — ripe but firm, cut in half; adds a subtle authentic sweetness

Fresh Vegetables

  • 1 small head cabbage — cut into wedges, core intact to keep leaves together
  • 1 bunch pechay (bok choy) — leaves separated, washed, stalks trimmed
  • 1 bunch green or long beans — ends trimmed, snapped in half

Seasoning & Serving

  • Salt — to taste, for final adjustments
  • Fish sauce & calamansi — served on the side for dipping

Method

Cleaning & Parboiling

10 min
  1. Place the pork chunks in a large pot, cover with water, and boil for 5 minutes.
  2. Drain the cloudy water and rinse the pork under cold water to wash off any scum. Clean the pot.

Simmering the Broth

45–60 min
  1. Return the cleaned pork to the pot. Add 1.5–2 liters fresh water, the quartered onion, peppercorns, and fish sauce.
  2. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook for 45–60 minutes (longer if using pata) until the pork is fork-tender.
  3. Periodically skim off any foam or excess oil to keep the broth clean.

Cooking the Starches

10–12 min
  1. Add the potatoes and saba bananas, if using. Cover and simmer about 10 minutes, until a fork easily pierces the potatoes.
  2. Taste the broth and adjust with a pinch of salt or another splash of fish sauce.

Steaming the Greens

3 min
  1. Add the green beans, cabbage wedges, and pechay stalks, placing the delicate leaves right on top.
  2. Do not stir — cover tightly and let steam for 2–3 minutes until the vegetables turn vibrant green but stay crisp. Turn off the heat.

To serve: ladle the hot soup, meat, and vegetables into large bowls with warm rice. Serve fish sauce with calamansi or crushed chili on the side for dipping.
