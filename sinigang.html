Pork Sinigang

Food Recipes

Description

Pork Sinigang is a classic Filipino sour soup that delivers a comforting balance of savory, tangy, and rich flavors. The heart of the dish relies on a souring agent—traditionally fresh, unripe tamarind (sampalok) or a convenient tamarind soup mix—which creates a sharp, mouth-watering broth. Succulent, marbled cuts of pork, such as pork belly (liempo), spare ribs, or shoulder, are simmered patiently alongside onions, tomatoes, and fish sauce until the meat becomes melt-in-your-mouth tender, rendering its fat into the broth to give it a deeply satisfying richness.

What makes Sinigang truly remarkable is its colorful medley of fresh, crisp vegetables that absorb the tangy soup. Long green chili peppers (siling haba) are tossed in to add a gentle, aromatic warmth without overwhelming heat, while a rustic combination of radish (labanos), string beans (sitaw), eggplant (talong), and okra introduces diverse textures. The dish is typically finished with a generous handful of fresh water spinach (kang kong) leaves just before serving. Best enjoyed piping hot, this soul-warming stew is traditionally ladled over a mountain of steamed white rice, often accompanied by a small side of fish sauce (patis) and crushed chili for an extra punch of umami.

    Ingredients

    The Meat and Broth Base

  • 1 kg (2.2 lbs) Pork - Use bone-in cuts like spare ribs, chops, or marbled pork belly (liempo) cut into 2-inch chunks.
  • 1.5 to 2 liters Water - Or rice washing (the water used to rinse rice) for a thicker, starchier broth.
    • 2 pieces Medium Red Onions - Quartered
  • 3 pieces Medium Tomatoes - Quartered.
  • 3 to 4 tbsp Fish Sauce (patis) - Adjust to taste for savory umami depth.

    • The Souring Agent

  • 1 pack (40g) Tamarind Soup Base Mix (Sinigang sa Sampalok mix) - Standard size. If using fresh tamarind, you will need about 10-15 pieces of green tamarind pods boiled and mashed to extract the juice.

    • The Vegetables and Aromatics

  • 1 medium Daikon Radish (labanos) - Peeled and sliced diagonally into 1/2-inch thick rounds.
  • 1 bunch String Beans (sitaw) - Cut into 2-inch long pieces.
  • 1 long Eggplant (talong) - Sliced diagonally into 1/2-inch thick pieces.
  • 6 to 8 pieces Okra - Tops trimmed off, left whole.
  • 2 to 3 pieces Long Green Chili (siling haba / siling pangsigang) - Kept whole to add aroma and mild warmth.
  • 1 bunch Water Spinach (kangkong) - Leaves and tender stems separated from the tough bottom stalks.
  • Salt and Black Pepper - To taste.

    Step-by-Step

    Phase 1: Prepping and Searing (10 minutes)

  1. Sear the meat (optional but recommended): In a large cooking pot over medium-high heat, add 1 tablespoon of cooking oil. Sear the pork chunks for 3–5 minutes until light brown on all sides. This locks in the juices.
  2. Add aromatics: Toss in your quartered onions and tomatoes. Sauté them with the pork for 2 minutes until they soften slightly and release their fragrance.

    3. Phase 2: Simmering the Pork (30 to 45 minutes)

  3. Boil the broth: Pour in 1.5 to 2 liters of water (or rice washing). Bring the liquid to a rolling boil.
  4. Skim the surface: Once boiling, reduce the heat to medium-low. Use a spoon or skimmer to scoop out any gray foam or impurities that rise to the top. This keeps your broth clear.
  5. Tenderize: Season with 3 tablespoons of fish sauce (patis) and a pinch of black pepper. Cover the pot and simmer for 30 to 45 minutes, or until the pork is fork-tender.

    6. Phase 3: Layering the Souring Agent and Hard Vegetables (10 minutes)

  6. Sour the soup: Pour in your tamarind soup mix pack. Stir well until completely dissolved. Taste the broth—if you want it more savory, add another tablespoon of fish sauce or a pinch of salt.
  7. Add slow-cooking veggies: Drop in the sliced radish (labanos) and the whole green chilies (siling haba). Cover and let simmer for 5 minutes. The chilies will release a distinct aroma.

    8. Phase 4: Finishes and Green Vegetables (5 minutes)

  8. Add medium veggies: Toss in the string beans (sitaw), okra, and eggplant (talong). Push them down into the broth, cover, and cook for another 3 to 4 minutes until they are tender but still vibrant.
  9. Wilting the greens: Add the tender stems and leaves of the water spinach (kangkong). Turn off the heat completely and cover the pot. Let the residual heat cook and wilt the greens for 1 to 2 minutes.

    10. Serve your Pork Sinigang piping hot in a deep bowl alongside a mountain of warm, steamed white rice. Have a small saucer of fish sauce and crushed chili on the side for dipping.
